Written before the design, the way a direct-response page is built. Notes in brackets say which job each

## Strategy

**Who we're talking to.** Mac users with years of files: freelancers, students, parents, small-business owners,
creators. Market sophistication is high: they have seen "organize your Mac" and "clean your Mac" apps for a
decade, so claims alone don't land. We lead with a mechanism they haven't heard and prove it on the page.

**Big Idea (Todd Brown: promise + mechanism + proof).**
- *Promise:* find any file by what you remember about it, then fix it in one right-click, privately.
- *Mechanism of the problem, "the Filename Trap":* your Mac files things by name, but names are written by
  cameras, scanners and download buttons (IMG_4021.HEIC, scan0003.pdf). You remember moments: who sent it,
  roughly when, what was in it. So the search box asks for the one thing you never knew.
- *Mechanism of the solution, "the Recall Index":* Haven builds a private index, on your Mac, of the five
  things memory actually keeps: what it was, when, where it came from, what's inside (even inside pictures),
  and what was said.
- *Proof:* working demos on the page, measured speed (300,000 files, slowest search 0.09 s), 209 automated
  tests on every build, a privacy design anyone can check (it works with Wi-Fi off), and a 30-day guarantee.

**Second problem, "the Upload Tax":** when you do find the file, small jobs (shrink, sign, merge, convert)
send you to converter sites that take your private files, or to a dozen apps that cost about $540 together.

**Offer.** $49 for 1 Mac, $79 for 3, $99 for 5. One payment, free version 1 updates, no subscription, no account,
no upsells. Risk reversal: the 30-Day "Find It or It's Free" Guarantee. No trial.

### The story [agitate with a scene the reader has lived]
**It's 9:58. The call is at 10.**
You need the contract Sam sent last week. You know it exists. You type "contract" into Finder and get
214 results with names like scan0047.pdf and Document (3).docx. You try Mail. Then Downloads. Then the
Desktop. At 10:06 you join the call flustered, with the wrong version open.

**It isn't you. It's the Filename Trap.**
Your Mac lists everything by name, and its search looks for the exact words you type. But the names were written
by your camera, your scanner and a download button. You never chose them, so you can't remember them. You
remember moments: who sent it, roughly when, what was on it. Type a word you remember and you get 214 results.

**And when you finally find it, the Upload Tax.**
It's too big to email. It needs a signature. It's six PDFs that should be one. So you search "compress video
free", upload a private file to a site you'd never heard of, and download it back with a watermark. Or you buy
another app. Bought one at a time, apps for these jobs cost about $540.

4. **What's inside:** words in PDFs and documents, and text inside screenshots and photos
5. **What was said:** words spoken in meetings, lectures and voice notes
