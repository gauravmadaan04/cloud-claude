The fair question

“Doesn't Spotlight do this already?” Partly. Here's exactly where.

Spotlight is good, and it's free. We'd rather show you the difference than oversell it. Pick something you'd look for:

Spotlight and Finder

Finds the words, not the inbox

Spotlight looks for “invoice” and “Sam” in names and text. That a file came by email is kept as a hidden “Where from” detail, which Finder can only search through its Other… list of attributes.

Haven

One sentence, one result

Reads “emailed” as From email and “last week” as a date, then looks for “invoice” and “Sam” inside the files. Chips show exactly how it read you, and any chip can be removed.

Spotlight and Finder

Doesn't hear recordings

Spotlight reads a recording's name and details, not the words spoken in it. Voice Memos and Notes can search transcripts of recordings made in those two apps.

Haven

Finds what was said

Transcribe any recording or video once, on your Mac, and its words become searchable like a document. “where someone said quarterly budget” finds the call.

Spotlight and Finder

Yes, this one it can do

Since macOS Ventura, Spotlight reads text in images too, as whole words, once it has worked through them in the background.

Haven

Also yes, with more ways in

Haven reads screenshots, photos and scans as they arrive, newest first. “wifi” finds “Wi-Fi”, and the same sentence can add a date, a size or the app it came from.

Spotlight and Finder

Possible, the long way

In a Finder search window, add a Kind rule, a Created date rule and a File size rule (found under Other…), one row at a time.

Haven

Type it the way you'd say it

“big videos older than a year” becomes three chips: Videos, Older than 1 year, Over 100 MB. Select them all and move them to the Trash, with Undo.

Spotlight and Finder

Only inside Photos

The Photos app finds duplicate photos and videos in its library. macOS has no built-in way to find duplicate files in your other folders.

Haven

Any folder you choose

Finds identical files and look-alike photos, picks the best copy to keep, and moves the rest to the Trash, with Undo.

Questions

Before you buy.

What's the catch?

There isn't one. You pay once and Haven is yours. No subscription, no account, no ads and nothing to upgrade inside the app. Every update to version 1 is free.

Why isn't there a free trial?

Because the guarantee is better than a trial. You get the full app from day one, and you have 30 days, twice a typical trial, to decide. If it isn't for you, we refund every cent.

Doesn't Spotlight already do this?

Partly. Spotlight finds words in names and text, and since macOS Ventura it reads text in images too. It doesn't hear recordings, understand “emailed” or “older than a year” in plain words, or find duplicate files outside Photos. See exactly where it differs.

Can I buy on my phone and install later?

Yes. Buy on any device. Your license key and the download link arrive by email, so you can install Haven on your Mac whenever you're ready. On an iPhone, opens the share sheet, so you can AirDrop it.

What happens when Haven 2 comes out?

Every update to version 1 is free. Buy during the launch offer and Haven 2 is included too, at no extra cost. Otherwise Haven 2 may be a paid upgrade, and the version you have keeps working.

Does Haven replace Finder?

No, it works alongside Finder. Your files stay in the folders they're already in. Haven indexes them so you can find them and work on them faster, and any file is one shortcut away from Finder.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Search, text recognition, transcription, background removal and every tool run on your Mac. Haven goes online only to download a speech model if you ask for transcription, check for updates and activate your license.

Could Haven delete something I need?

Haven never deletes anything outright. Clean Up shows you every file first, everything goes to the Trash, and Undo brings it back. Tools never change your originals; they make new files next to them.

Will it slow my Mac down?

No. Haven reads your folders in the background at low priority, a few files at a time, so your Mac stays responsive. Its index is small: about 18 MB for 300,000 files.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel. Translate needs macOS 15, and Summarize needs Apple Intelligence on macOS 26.

How do the 3 and 5 Mac licenses work?

One license key works on that many Macs at the same time, yours or the people you live or work with. Moving to a new Mac? Deactivate the old one in Settings and enter your key on the new one.
